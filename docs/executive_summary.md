# Executive summary: Kestrel Health Services workforce outlook, FY2027–FY2029

> **Fictional organisation.** KHS internal figures are **synthetic** case-study data. External labour-market facts are **real** (ABS, JSA, WA Public Sector Commission) and are cited in brackets. Model outputs depend on assumptions listed in `forecasting_and_scenarios.md`; several are illustrative.

## The answer in one paragraph

Under baseline assumptions KHS can roughly hold its position. The shortage falls from 46 FTE in FY2027 to 28 FTE in FY2029 (1.5% of demand), and agency spend stays near $4–5M a year. That outcome depends on sustaining about 350 hires a year and holding attrition at about 14.5%. The labour market is still tighter than before COVID [WA vacancies per unemployed person 0.56 vs 0.24 in 2015–19, ABS], and the uncertainty is skewed toward worse outcomes (P90 shortage 111 FTE).

If attrition returns to FY2023 levels and shortage occupations fill at the national rate [57% vs 78%, JSA OSL 2026], the shortage reaches **182 FTE by FY2029**, 3-year agency spend rises from $13M to **$48M**, and about 250 FTE-years of care go unstaffed. The gap is concentrated in **Care Workers, Medical Practitioners, Allied Health and regional sites**.

We recommend funding retention and regional recruitment capacity now. Agency procurement should be treated as a cost lever, not a supply lever.

## Findings

**1. KHS does not yet have one reliable number for its workforce, and the raw figures point the wrong way.**
The raw HRIS extract overstates headcount by 3.4% and FTE by 6.2%. At 1,728.5 FTE it shows KHS *above* its 1,701.8 FTE establishment, when the reconciled position is **74.3 FTE vacant (4.4%)**. About 80 FTE of vacant establishment has no live requisition, so it will not be filled. *[S; data_quality_report.md]*
→ **Action:** adopt the reconciled HRIS–payroll–establishment view as the single source for workforce reporting, and require a requisition or a formal hold decision for every vacant position.

**2. Medical Practitioners and Allied Health are the highest-risk families, because KHS cannot recruit them fast enough.**
- Median time-to-fill is 101 days for doctors and 65 days for Allied Health (P90 213 and 135 days). *[S]*
- Both are rated in national shortage, and WA online ads are 189% and 168% above 2019 levels. *[R: JSA OSL 2026, JSA IVI Sep 2026]*
- In FY2027, required medical hires are 149% of demonstrated capacity. Doctors account for $2.7M of the $6.6M FY2026 agency spend. *[S]*

→ **Action:** recruit medical and allied roles on a 4–6-month lead time (open requisitions on resignation notice, not on departure); establish a preferred locum panel; build registrar and new-graduate allied pipelines with WA universities.

**3. Care Workers have the highest turnover, and under pressure they become the largest gap.**
FY2026 turnover is 29.5%, and 38.8% of new care workers leave within a year. *[S]* Under the Constraint scenario Care Workers make up 77 of the 182 FTE shortage. Nationally, Aged and Disabled Carers (4231) are in shortage. *[R]*
→ **Action:** target first-year retention (structured onboarding, buddy shifts, rostering stability) and track early attrition monthly.

**4. Regional sites, Broome most of all, carry structurally higher risk.**
- Broome: vacancy 14.0%, fill rate 73.7%, turnover 21.9%, median time-to-fill 60 days (P90 164). *[S]*
- WA regional health workers are older: 27.7% are aged 55+ in Rest of WA, against 22.9% statewide. *[R: Census 2021]*
- In the option testing, a 30% uplift in regional recruitment capacity cut the Constraint FY2029 shortage from 182 to 139 FTE. It had the lowest programme cost per FTE-year avoided ($8k), *based on illustrative cost and effect assumptions*.

→ **Action:** fund a regional recruitment pilot (a dedicated recruiter, relocation and housing support) at Broome and Kalgoorlie, with an evaluation built in.

**5. Registered Nurses are in balance overall but in the wrong places.**
Graduate placements and EN→RN conversion keep RN supply in line with demand in the Baseline (0 FTE shortage). However, Perth South is 14.1 FTE over its RN establishment while Kalgoorlie is 4.8 FTE under. *[S]* RNs remain a national shortage occupation, with WA ads up 93% on 2019. *[R]* Under Constraint, an RN shortage of 21 FTE emerges.
→ **Action:** redeploy rather than recruit. Allocate part of the graduate intake and rotational placements to regional sites, and protect the EN→RN pathway.

**6. Recruitment capacity and attrition decide the outcome; agency pricing does not.**
- In the sensitivity analysis, ±20% recruitment capacity moves the FY2029 shortage between 13 and 95 FTE, and ±20% attrition between 14 and 75 FTE.
- A cheaper agency panel saves an estimated $5.7M over three years under Constraint but changes the shortage by 0 FTE.
- Wage growth of ±1pp changes 3-year cost by about ±$16M without affecting supply. *[MODEL]*

→ **Action:** plan talent-acquisition resourcing from the modelled hiring requirement (about 350–380 hires a year in the Baseline; 480–570 needed under Constraint against about 394 possible).

**7. Prepare for the downside, with explicit triggers.**
The Monte Carlo median FY2029 shortage (47 FTE) is above the Baseline (28), and P90 is 111 FTE. *[MODEL]*
→ **Action:** switch to the Constraint playbook if two or more triggers fire for two consecutive quarters:
- rolling 12-month turnover above 16%;
- median time-to-fill above 50 days;
- the WA vacancy/unemployment ratio rising above 0.7 [ABS];
- WA health IVI rising above 20% year-on-year [JSA].

## Recruit, train, redeploy, automate or contract?

| Situation (evidence) | Response |
|---|---|
| Shortage occupation, long time-to-fill, national shortage (Medical, Allied) | **Recruit early** + **contract** (locum panel) as a bridge; build pipelines |
| High turnover, short time-to-fill (Care Workers) | **Retain** first; recruit volume continuously |
| Balanced overall, uneven by site (RNs) | **Redeploy** / rotate; allocate graduates regionally |
| Long-run supply gap with an internal feeder (EN → RN) | **Train** (grow your own); results arrive after 2–3 years |
| Administrative work, low shortage (Admin: OSL no shortage, 0.94% projected growth) | **Automate** and streamline; restrict back-fills |
| Short-term peaks, leave cover | **Contract / agency**, managed through a panel to control price |

## Decisions requested

1. Endorse the reconciled workforce dataset as the official source (no additional cost).
2. Approve a 12-month regional recruitment pilot and a first-year retention programme for Care Workers and clinical staff, each with pre-agreed evaluation metrics.
3. Approve talent-acquisition resourcing for about 380 hires a year, with the Constraint triggers above.
4. Commission the internal data listed in `limitations_and_next_steps.md` so the model can be rerun on real KHS data before FY2027 budget finalisation.
